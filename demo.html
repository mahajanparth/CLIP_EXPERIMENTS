PCAKeep the widest directions

PCA finds the two (or, in 3-D, three) directions along which the points spread out most, and drops the rest of the 512. It is linear and gives the same answer every time, so straight-line distances still mean something.

The cost: two axes hold only a fraction of the variance, so points that look close may differ along a dropped direction.

t-SNEKeep each point’s neighbours

t-SNE turns distances into “who is my neighbour” probabilities, then moves points around in 2-D until those probabilities match. Perplexity is roughly how many neighbours each point pays attention to.

Tight clusters are real. Gaps between clusters, cluster sizes, and the overall layout are not. Press “New random start” and the same data comes out arranged differently.

GapImages and text sit apart

With raw embeddings, images and sentences form two separate clouds, and PCA’s first axis mostly splits them. In both data sets every point’s nearest neighbour is from its own modality. Image–image cosines average about 0.5; the best image–caption match here is only 0.38.

Matching still works because CLIP only compares an image against texts. Ticking “Remove modality gap” subtracts each modality’s mean vector; captions then land beside their photos. See Liang et al., Mind the Gap, NeurIPS 2022.
