CLIP’s Blind Spots

CLIP is good at telling you what is in a picture. It is much worse at how many, where, and which colour goes with which object. These 90 drawings each come with a correct caption and a word-swapped wrong one, and CLIP has to pick. At the bottom you can run CLIP on your own images and captions, right in this browser.

The verdictThree simple tests, three failures

Each square is one drawing: green when CLIP scores the correct caption higher, red when it prefers the swapped one. Spatial and colour drawings come in mirrored pairs, grouped together.

The evidenceEvery drawing, every score

Numbers are cosine similarities between the image and each caption. Click any drawing to load it into the tester below.

Why it happensThe text encoder barely hears the difference

Before the image is even involved, CLIP’s text encoder maps the correct caption and its swapped twin to almost the same vector. “A red cube to the left of a blue sphere” and “…to the right of…” have an average cosine of 0.995. Same words, same embedding.

Contrastive training only teaches CLIP to tell a caption apart from the other captions in the batch. Web captions in a batch almost never differ only in word order or a left/right swap, so nothing pushes the model to encode it. It learns something close to a bag of words.

Counting is partly learned (number words do appear in captions), but CLIP drifts toward “two”. Benchmarks built on this idea include Winoground (Thrush et al., CVPR 2022) and ARO (Yuksekgonul et al., ICLR 2023).
